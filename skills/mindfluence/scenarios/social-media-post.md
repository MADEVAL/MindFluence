# Scenario: Social Media Post

> **Anti-patterns:** `SKILL.md` → Anti-Patterns section for detection rules.
> **Cultural:** `SKILL.md` → Cultural Quick-Reference for region-specific adjustments.

## CONTEXT
Writing a post for social media - short-form (Twitter/X, Threads) or medium-form (LinkedIn, Instagram caption, Telegram).

## GOAL
Maximize engagement (likes, comments, shares, saves) and drive a specific action: click link, subscribe, comment, think differently, or buy.

---

## PLATFORM-SPECIFIC BIAS STRATEGIES

### Twitter/X / Threads (≤280 chars or short thread)
**Dominant biases:** Availability (#6), Framing (#3), False Consensus (#14), Social Proof (#1)

**Patterns:**
- **The Hot Take** - `[Framing (#3) + False Consensus (#14)]` State a bold, polarizing opinion as if everyone secretly agrees. "Unpopular opinion: [widely held belief in your niche]." Drives quote-tweets.
- **The Stat Bombshell** - `[Anchoring (#2) + Fear (#5)]` "The average [person] loses $X/year to [problem]. Here's how to stop." One stat = one anchor.
- **The Contrarian Thread** - `[Confirmation Bias (#7) + In-Group (#15)]` "Everything you've been told about X is wrong. 🧵" Each tweet in thread: one bias-driven claim.
- **The «I was wrong»** - `[Bias Blind Spot (#19) + Authority (#4)]` "I believed [misconception] for 10 years. Here's what I know now." Vulnerability = massive engagement.

> ⚠ **AP-3:** Framing needs an anchor. **AP-2:** Fear hooks need a solution exit in the body. **AP-1:** Social proof requires specific numbers. **AP-10:** Contrarian threads - make the reader feel smart for agreeing, not wrong.

### LinkedIn
**Dominant biases:** Authority (#4), Social Proof (#1), Sunk Cost (#12), Halo Effect (#16)

**Patterns:**
- **The Lesson Learned** - Story (Availability (#6)) → Mistake (Bias Blind Spot (#19)) → Lesson (Framing (#3)) → Takeaway for reader.
- **The Case Study** - "How [client/company] achieved [result] in [timeframe]." Survivorship Bias (#9) + Authority (#4). Specifics are mandatory.
- **The Framework Dump** - "The 3-part framework I use for [outcome]." Authority (#4) + Anchoring (#2) (your method = the reference). Image carousel = halo effect (#16).
- **The Data Revelation** - "We analyzed X data points. Here's what surprised us." Authority (#4) + Availability (#6). The surprise = the engagement hook.

> ⚠ **AP-4:** Authority needs named sources ("studies show" = FAIL). **AP-1:** Social proof must have specific numbers + names + results. **AP-7:** Sunk cost is late-stage only - not for first contact. **AP-9:** Halo source must match audience.

### Instagram / Visual Platforms
**Dominant biases:** Halo Effect (#16), Social Proof (#1), Endowment Effect (#10), Availability (#6)

**Patterns:**
- **The Transformation** - Before/After visual. Survivorship Bias (#9). Specifics in caption.
- **The Carousel Deep-Dive** - Swipe-through educational content. Each slide = one bias. Halo from design quality.
- **The Relatable Story** - Personal, emotional story. Fundamental Attribution Error (#11) - "I struggled too. It wasn't my fault. Here's what changed."

> ⚠ **AP-1:** Social proof needs specific metrics, not vague claims. **AP-9:** Halo from design quality must match audience's taste level. **AP-11:** Stories need ≥3 concrete sensory details.

### Telegram / Newsletter
**Dominant biases:** In-Group (#15), Confirmation (#7), Sunk Cost (#12), Status Quo (#13)

**Patterns:**
- **The Insider Update** - "Here's what I'm seeing that nobody's talking about." In-Group (#15) + False Consensus (#14).
- **The Curated Roundup** - "5 things worth your attention this week." Status Quo (#13, familiar format) + Authority (#4, your curation = your taste).

> ⚠ **AP-8:** In-group needs named identity + shared experience + out-group. **AP-10:** Confirmation - make reader feel smart for their existing beliefs. **AP-7:** Sunk cost valid only after sustained readership.

## POST STRUCTURE (universal)

### HOOK (line 1, visible before "read more")
One of:
- **Number:** "I lost $47,000 before learning this."
- **Contradiction:** "The best marketers are terrible at marketing themselves."
- **Question:** "What if everything you know about [topic] is backwards?"
- **Vivid image:** "Picture this: it's 3 AM. You're staring at the ceiling because..."
- **Fear trigger:** "Here's what's silently killing your [metric]."

> ⚠ **AP-2:** Fear triggers in hooks need a solution within the body. **AP-11:** "Picture this" hooks need ≥3 concrete sensory details for the brain to visualize.

### BODY
- **Paragraph 1:** Expand the hook. Make it personal. (Availability (#6))
- **Paragraph 2:** Introduce the insight / framework / lesson. (Authority (#4) / Framing (#3))
- **Paragraph 3:** Support with example or data. (Social Proof (#1) / Anchoring (#2))
- **Paragraph 4:** Connect to reader. "If you're [identity], this matters because..." (False Consensus (#14) / In-Group (#15))

### CTA
Never use generic "Like and subscribe." Each CTA is bias-informed:
- **Comment bait:** "Agree or disagree? I read every response." (False Consensus (#14) + Social Proof (#1) - comments = social proof for the post)
- **Save bait:** "Save this for the next time you [situation]." (Endowment (#10) - they now "own" your content)
- **Share bait:** "Tag someone who needs to hear this." (In-Group (#15) + Social Proof (#1))
- **Link click:** "I wrote a deeper breakdown. Link in bio/comments." (Sunk Cost (#12) - they've read this far)
- **DM/Subscribe:** "I share insights like this every week. Subscribe so you don't miss the next one." (Loss Aversion (#5) + Status Quo (#13) - don't break the habit)

> ⚠ **AP-7:** Sunk cost ("you've read this far") - valid only on long-form content. **AP-5:** Give value in primary content, pitch in a separate communication. **AP-6:** Scarcity/urgency must be genuine and explained.

## ENGAGEMENT HACKS (bias-driven)

1. **The Poll** - "Which is harder: A or B?" (False Consensus (#14) - everyone wants to see if others agree)
2. **The Hot-Take Reply** - Reply to a large account with a smart counterpoint. (Authority (#4) + In-Group (#15) - you're "one of the smart ones")
3. **The Dissonance Question** - "You say [value] is your priority. But your actions say otherwise. Why?" (Cognitive Dissonance (#8) - demands resolution)
4. **The Vulnerability Arc** - "I was terrified to post this. Here's why I did anyway." (Bias Blind Spot (#19) + Authority (#4) - owning your bias = ultimate credibility)

> ⚠ **AP-12:** Dissonance questions must blame the situation/system, not the person. "The algorithm makes it hard" not "You say you value it but you don't."
